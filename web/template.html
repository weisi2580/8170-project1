CMP_SC 8170 · Project 1 · 3 CASP15 targets × 2 MSA conditions · AlphaFold 3 Server

Does a hand-built MSA beat AlphaFold 3's own alignment?

Not on these three targets. AF3's default MSA matched our custom ColabFold/MMseqs2 MSA on the easy target and beat it on the other two. The hard target, where a custom MSA was supposed to help most, had no homologs to find in either pipeline.

Default MSA ≥ custom on all 3 targets. ΔTM (custom − default) = −0.003, −0.038, −0.087. On T1112 and T1122, all 5 samples of one condition beat all 5 of the other.

AF3 simply found more. Its server MSA held 8,032 vs 581 sequences (T1183) and 1,967 vs 855 (T1112). Our ≥ 50% coverage filter also dropped fragments AF3 keeps.

The orphan target had nothing to find. For T1122 both MSAs are the query plus a self-hit (AF3's also has one 37-residue fragment): Neff/L < 0.01 either way.

Confidence picked the wrong one. The custom models had the higher pLDDT and pTM on T1183 and T1112 while being less accurate.

How it was measured

  • Custom MSA: ColabFold MMseqs2 server (UniRef + environmental DB), filtered to ≥ 50% coverage, ≥ 20% identity, < 90% redundancy, then uploaded to AF3 Server.
  • Default: same sequence, AF3 builds its own MSA. Templates on in both; 5 samples per job.
  • Scored on the CASP15 evaluation unit with US-align (residue-index TM-score, Cα RMSD) and Cα-lDDT (15 Å, 0.5/1/2/4 Å).

What limits the conclusion

  • Three targets: a case study, not a statistical test.
  • Each job used its own random seed, and AF3 retrieved different templates for T1112 and T1122, so the custom condition changes more than the MSA.
  • On the server a custom MSA replaces AF3's MSA; a union of both was not tested.

Next experiments

  • Rerun both conditions with templates off to isolate the MSA.
  • Relax the coverage filter for T1112 and check whether the C-terminal region (residues 340–425) recovers.
  • Add a "custom ∪ default" MSA condition.
